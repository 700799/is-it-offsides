    Meet the assistant referee

    There is a fourth colour on every pitch in this quiz: a yellow figure jogging the touchline with a flag. That is the assistant referee, and where they stand is not a matter of taste.

    • They stand on the offside line. The guidelines put the AR level with the second-last defender — or level with the ball, once the ball is nearer the goal line than the defence. Watch them slide up and down as the defence pushes out. In scenario 5 you will see them abandon the defenders and follow the ball instead. That is why: the offside line is whichever of the two is further forward.
    • Square-on, not from behind. Standing level means judging the line at ninety degrees. From any other angle, a flat back four looks staggered — which is exactly why the view from your sofa disagrees with the flag.
    • The flag is sometimes late on purpose. On a tight call with a likely goalscoring chance, the AR uses the wait-and-see technique: flag stays down, the move plays out, and only then does it go up. A wrong flag kills a legitimate attack forever; a late flag costs nothing. Clear offsides are still flagged immediately. Scenario 11 is built on this.
    • And now there is help. Under the 2026/27 Laws, competitions are permitted to use technology — including semi-automated offside technology — to help the referee make or change an offside decision. It changes who draws the line, not where the line is. Every answer in this quiz is the same with or without it.

    In this quiz the flag always stays down while the call is yours. It only goes up once you have answered — no spoilers.
